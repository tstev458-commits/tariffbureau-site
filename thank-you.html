Step 02 · Most Important Document

Understanding CBP Form 4811

Form 4811 is the legal mechanism that makes The Tariff Bureau’s end-to-end recovery model work. Read this before you sign anything.

CBP FORM 4811

Special Address Notification — Refund Designation

CBP Form 4811 allows the Importer of Record to designate a third party — The Tariff Bureau LLC — as the notify party to receive refund payments on behalf of the IOR.

This is not a power of attorney. It does not transfer ownership of your refund. It instructs CBP to route the ACH refund to The Tariff Bureau’s designated bank account, after which we deduct our fee and wire your net balance to you.

How Form 4811 Works — Step by Step
1
You Sign

Execute Form 4811

You sign the Form 4811 designating The Tariff Bureau LLC as notify party for your IEEPA refund entries. One signature covers all your entries.

2
We File

CAPE Declaration Submitted to CBP

Our licensed customs broker partner files your CAPE Declaration through the ACE Secure Data Portal. Form 4811 is filed simultaneously.

3
CBP Processes

60–90 Day CBP Processing Window

CBP validates your CAPE Declaration, confirms liquidation status, and calculates your refund including statutory interest (7% individual / 6% corporate per 19 CFR 24.36).

4
CBP Pays

ACH Refund Sent to The Tariff Bureau

CBP issues the consolidated ACH refund directly to The Tariff Bureau LLC’s Mercury bank account as designated in Form 4811.

5
We Distribute

Fee Deducted — Net Wired to You

Within 5 business days of receiving CBP’s ACH deposit, The Tariff Bureau deducts the agreed contingency fee and wires your net balance to your designated bank account.

The Tariff Bureau Contingency (12%)- $24,000
$1,500 Screening Fee Credit Applied- $1,500

False Claims Act Notice: Form 4811 does not transfer liability for the accuracy of your CAPE Declaration. You remain solely responsible for certifying that all entry data is accurate. Filing with valuation errors, misclassification, or incorrect origin data can trigger enforcement.
