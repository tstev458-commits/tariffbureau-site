Understanding CBP Form 4811

Form 4811 is the legal mechanism that makes The Tariff Bureau's end-to-end recovery model work. Read this before you sign anything.

CBP FORM 4811

Special Address Notification — Refund Designation

CBP Form 4811 is a standard U.S. Customs and Border Protection form that allows the Importer of Record to designate a third party — in this case, The Tariff Bureau LLC — as the notify party to receive refund payments, notices of liquidation, and duty bills on behalf of the IOR.

This is not a power of attorney. It does not transfer ownership of your refund. It simply instructs CBP to route the ACH refund payment to The Tariff Bureau's designated bank account, after which we deduct our fee and wire your net balance to you.

How Form 4811 Works — Step by Step
1
You Sign

Execute Form 4811

You sign the Form 4811 designating The Tariff Bureau LLC as the notify party for your IEEPA refund entries. This is included in your Engagement Letter package — one signature covers all your entries.

2
We File

CAPE Declaration Submitted to CBP

Our licensed customs broker partner files your CAPE Declaration — the CSV of your eligible entry numbers — through the ACE Secure Data Portal. Form 4811 is filed simultaneously, updating CBP's records.

3
CBP Processes

60–90 Day CBP Processing Window

CBP validates your CAPE Declaration against entry records, confirms liquidation status, and calculates your refund including statutory interest (7% individual / 6% corporate per 19 CFR 24.36). You will receive a CBP notice of acceptance.

4
CBP Pays

ACH Refund Sent to The Tariff Bureau

CBP issues the consolidated ACH refund directly to The Tariff Bureau LLC's Mercury bank account as designated in Form 4811. The refund arrives as a single lump-sum payment covering all accepted entries.

5
We Distribute

Fee Deducted — Net Wired to You

Within 5 business days of receiving CBP's ACH deposit, The Tariff Bureau deducts the agreed contingency fee and wires your net balance to your designated bank account. You receive a detailed disbursement statement.

Example Distribution — $200,000 Refund at 12% Contingency

Total CBP ACH Refund Received $200,000
The Tariff Bureau Contingency (12%) - $24,000
$1,500 Screening Fee Credit Applied - $1,500
Your Net Distribution (wired within 5 business days) $174,500

False Claims Act Notice: Form 4811 does not transfer liability for the accuracy of your CAPE Declaration. You remain solely responsible for certifying that all entry data is accurate. CBP and the DOJ–DHS Trade Fraud Task Force actively monitor CAPE filings. Filing with valuation errors, misclassification, or incorrect origin data — even unintentionally — can trigger enforcement. This is why The Tariff Bureau's pre-submission audit exists.
