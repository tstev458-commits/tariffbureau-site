Critical developments — week of May 19, 2026
CAPE Live Treasury ACH payments began May 12 — first refunds are flowing
CBP launched Phase 1 of the CAPE portal on April 20, 2026 — the same day The Tariff Bureau opened its doors. As of May 11, 8.3 million entries have been liquidated or reliquidated without IEEPA duties, and the U.S. Treasury began issuing ACH payments as early as May 12. Importers must have ACH bank information registered in their ACE Portal account or refunds will be automatically rejected. As of the latest CBP filing, 1,880 consolidated refunds are currently blocked solely due to missing ACH enrollment — money owed that importers haven't claimed.
SCOTUS Ruling Supreme Court struck IEEPA tariffs 6–3 in landmark February decision
In Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump (Feb. 20, 2026), the Court held the President lacks statutory authority under IEEPA to impose blanket tariffs. All reciprocal and fentanyl tariffs — covering imports from virtually every country since April 2025 — are invalid as of inception. Justice Kavanaugh's dissent acknowledged the government "may be required to refund billions of dollars" and warned the process could be a logistical "mess." Over 2,000 individual CIT lawsuits seeking refunds have since been filed, including by major U.S. corporations.
New Tariff Regime Section 122 tariff: 10–15% global rate · Hard expiration July 24, 2026
Immediately after the SCOTUS ruling, the administration invoked Section 122 of the 1974 Trade Act to impose a 10% global tariff effective February 24, 2026. Trump subsequently announced an increase to the 15% statutory cap. Critically, Section 122 carries a hard 150-day limit — it expires on or around July 24, 2026 unless Congress passes legislation to extend it. A coalition of 24 states has challenged Section 122 in the CIT, arguing it exceeds the law's scope. On May 12, a federal appeals court issued an administrative stay, temporarily pausing a lower court's decision to block the tariff while litigation continues.
Active Litigation 2,500+ CIT cases filed · Government appeal deadline: early June 2026
Over 2,500 individual IEEPA tariff cases were pending at the Court of International Trade as of March 2026, with new filings continuing. New Section 122 challenges have been filed, including Burlap & Barrel, Inc. v. Trump (Case No. 26-01606) by the Liberty Justice Center on March 9. The government's deadline to appeal the CIT's nationwide IEEPA refund order runs through early June 2026. A successful appeal could stay the refund mechanism entirely and significantly delay all payments — making professional claim management and protest-filing essential.
Phase 2 Pending Older entries and complex scenarios not yet covered — deadlines are running
Phase 1 covers only unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation. Entries with antidumping or countervailing duties, warehouse entries, and entries liquidated more than 80 days ago are excluded and awaiting a future phase. CBP has not announced a Phase 2 date. Importers with older entries must file CIT protests within 180 days of liquidation to preserve eligibility — that clock is already ticking on entries from 2025. Firms are advised to audit all affected entries now and establish a protest schedule before deadlines expire.
Forward Threat Section 301 investigations launched against 16 economies including China and EU
On March 11, 2026, the USTR initiated Section 301 investigations into structural excess capacity in 16 economies. A parallel forced-labor investigation covers 60 countries. These are entirely separate from IEEPA and could produce a new wave of additional tariff exposure — meaning importers recovering IEEPA refunds may simultaneously be accumulating new liabilities. With Section 122 also set to expire or be replaced, the tariff landscape through Q3–Q4 2026 remains highly volatile.
