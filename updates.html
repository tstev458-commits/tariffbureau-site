DOJ Signals Federal Circuit Appeal — Finally Liquidated Entries Now at Serious Risk

On May 29, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice filed a motion signaling its intent to appeal the CIT's universal IEEPA refund injunction to the Federal Circuit Court of Appeals. In the same motion, CBP reversed its prior position and asserted — for the first time — that it lacks statutory authority to reliquidate "finally liquidated" entries (those more than 90 days past liquidation) without an importer-specific court order. Judge Eaton denied the DOJ motion and ordered CBP Commissioner Rodney Scott to appear in person on June 9, 2026 to explain the agency's compliance timeline. The DOJ appeal deadline is June 6, 2026. An appeal to the Federal Circuit is now widely expected before then.

🚨 Deadline Alert June 6, 2026 • DOJ / Federal Circuit

DOJ Federal Circuit Appeal Deadline — Tomorrow

The U.S. Department of Justice has until June 6, 2026 to file its appeal of the CIT's universal injunction with the Federal Circuit Court of Appeals. Based on DOJ's May 29 filing, this appeal is widely expected. DOJ will argue the CIT exceeded its authority by issuing a "universal injunction" covering non-litigant importers on finally liquidated entries. ⚖️ Section 122 Struck Down May 20, 2026 • Court of International Trade

CIT Strikes Down Section 122 Global 10% Tariff — New Refund Pool Opening

On May 20, 2026, the Court of International Trade ruled that the 10% global Section 122 tariffs imposed February 24, 2026 are invalid. Section 122 is a balance-of-payments provision and the Court found the statutory conditions were not met. Importers who paid the 10% Section 122 tariff from February 24 through the ruling date now have a separate potential refund claim entirely distinct from IEEPA. The administration is expected to appeal and may accelerate Section 232 replacement measures before the July 24 expiration.

⚡ New Advisory Opportunity

Section 122 refund claims follow a different CBP path than CAPE. The Tariff Bureau is evaluating Section 122 advisory services for clients with significant post-February 24 imports. If this applies to you, flag it in your intake.

📋 Legislative May 2026 • U.S. Senate • S.3905

Tariff Refund Act of 2026 (S.3905) — Legislative Backstop Independent of Appeal

Senate bill S.3905, co-sponsored by Senator Hickenlooper (CO) and a bipartisan coalition, would require CBP to refund all IEEPA duties within 180 days of enactment with statutory interest — and create a priority queue for small businesses. Critically, this legislation would function as a backstop independent of the Federal Circuit appeal. A successful DOJ appeal would not defeat the bill's mandate if enacted. A separate coalition of 26 Senators wrote to CBP Commissioner Scott calling for fully automated refunds using CBP's existing data, bypassing the CAPE opt-in process entirely.

Section 301 & 232 Now Anchor U.S. Tariff Architecture — Permanent Exposure for Importers

The post-IEEPA tariff landscape is organized around three permanent authorities: Section 122 (expiring July 24, now struck down), Section 232 as the national security tool, and Section 301 as the country/policy unfair-trade tool. Effective landed duty on China-origin goods now routinely exceeds 50%.
