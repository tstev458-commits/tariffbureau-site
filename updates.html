IEEPA Refunds Are Hitting Bank Accounts — $35.46 Billion Approved

As of May 12, U.S. importers are receiving ACH deposits directly from the federal government. One small business owner confirmed a $10,000 refund posted at 7 a.m. — covering the 20% IEEPA tariff paid on Chinese imports — with an additional $10,000 expected for remaining entries. Major retailers including Walmart, Target, Nike, Gap, and Home Depot are set to receive significant compensation. Flexport confirmed its clients received $137 million in aggregate refunds as of Monday. The first phase covers entries processed within the past 80 days, with subsequent phases to follow.

Critical deadline in effect: The 180-day liquidation finality clock is running on every entry. Once an entry passes that window without a filed CAPE declaration or protest, it becomes permanently ineligible for refund — no exceptions. CBP projects 60–90 day processing once a claim is submitted; incomplete submissions cause delays and rejection.

CBP's CAPE portal consolidates refunds across all entries rather than processing individually — meaning importers with thousands of entries file a single declaration. Classification errors, valuation inconsistencies, or origin documentation gaps can result in claim rejection and False Claims Act exposure. Professional review before submission is not optional for $250K+ claims.

Legal & Policy Developments
May 12, 2026 Legal

Appeals Court Issues Temporary Stay on 10% Global Tariff Ruling

A federal appeals court temporarily paused a lower court decision that had blocked President Trump's 10% global tariff — imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974. The stay keeps the 10% tariff in force while litigation continues.

What this means for importers: The Section 122 global tariff remains in force for now. This tariff is separately scheduled to expire July 24, 2026 unless extended by Congress — regardless of the court outcome. Importers should plan for continued tariff uncertainty through at least Q3 2026 and model both expiry and extension scenarios.

Note: Section 122 tariffs are distinct from IEEPA tariffs and are not eligible for refund under the CAPE program. Only IEEPA reciprocal tariffs imposed between April 5, 2025 and February 24, 2026 qualify for CAPE refund recovery.

CAPE Portal Operations
May 6, 2026 CBP Operations CAPE Portal

CBP Issues Technical Corrections — ACE Monitoring Reports Now Live

CBP published CSMS #68554727 on May 6, providing technical corrections to Section 232 duties on aluminum, steel, and copper. Separately, CBP confirmed that ACE monitoring reports are now available for importers and licensed brokers to track real-time CAPE refund claim status.

Since the April 20 CAPE launch, Phase 1 has been limited to certain unliquidated entries and entries that liquidated within the past 80 days. Entries liquidated outside that window require a separate protest filing — a pathway many importers are not aware of and may be missing.

Two filing paths exist: CAPE Declaration (Phase 1, within 80-day window) and CBP Protest (entries outside the window). Most importers need to evaluate both. Filing the wrong path results in rejection without appeal opportunity.
