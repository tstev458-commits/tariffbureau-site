IEEPA Refunds Are Hitting Bank Accounts — $35.46 Billion Approved

The wait is over. As of May 12, U.S. importers are receiving actual ACH deposits from the federal government. One small business owner confirmed a $10,000 refund hit her account at 7 a.m. — covering the 20% IEEPA tariff she paid on goods imported from China — with another $10,000 payment expected for additional entries.

Major retailers including Walmart, Target, Nike, Gap, and Home Depot are among those set to receive compensation. Flexport confirmed its clients received $137 million in aggregate refunds as of Monday.

Critical deadline: The 180-day liquidation finality clock is running on every entry. Once an entry passes that window without a filed CAPE declaration or protest, it becomes permanently ineligible for refund — no exceptions. CBP projects 60–90 day processing once a claim is submitted, with potential delays for incomplete filings.

CBP's CAPE portal consolidates refunds rather than processing on an entry-by-entry basis — meaning importers with thousands of entries file a single declaration. Errors or incomplete submissions cause delays; professional review before submission is essential.

Legal & Policy Developments
May 12, 2026 Legal

Appeals Court Issues Temporary Stay on 10% Global Tariff Ruling

A federal appeals court temporarily paused a lower court decision that had blocked President Trump's 10% global tariff — currently imposed under Section 122 of the 1974 Trade Act. The stay keeps the 10% tariff in effect while litigation continues.

What this means: The Section 122 global tariff remains in force for now. Separately, under the law, this tariff is scheduled to expire on July 24, 2026 unless extended by Congress — regardless of the court outcome. Importers should plan for continued uncertainty on post-IEEPA tariff exposure through at least Q3 2026.

CAPE Portal Operations
May 6, 2026 CAPE Portal

CBP Issues Technical Corrections + ACE Monitoring Reports Now Available

CBP published CSMS #68554727 on May 6, providing technical corrections to Section 232 duties on aluminum, steel, and copper. Separately, CBP confirmed that multiple ACE reports are now available for importers and brokers to monitor the status of CAPE refund claims in real time.

Since the April 20 CAPE launch, Phase 1 has been limited to certain unliquidated entries and entries that have liquidated within the past 80 days. Entries liquidated outside that window require a separate protest filing — a step many importers are missing.
