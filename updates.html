29 Days to June 7 Appeal Deadline — CAPE Acceptance Is the Threshold That Matters

The government's window to appeal the February 24 court order expires on or around June 7, 2026. A successful stay or appeal could halt the processing of new CAPE Declarations mid-stream. Importers who have obtained CAPE Declaration acceptance before that date are meaningfully better protected than those still in queue.

CBP is currently processing Phase 1 declarations. Processing times vary by claim size and entry complexity — large claims ($250K+) are subject to mandatory review that can extend timelines by 3–4 weeks. If you have not yet filed, the effective deadline to start is now.

Action Begin intake immediately. CAPE Declaration acceptance — not filing — is the milestone that provides protection before June 7.
May 2 2026
CBP Advisory
CBP Issues Supplemental CAPE Guidance: Phase 2 Eligibility Criteria Clarified

U.S. Customs and Border Protection released supplemental guidance clarifying Phase 2 CAPE eligibility — covering entries filed under IEEPA between October 2024 and the April 5, 2025 tariff schedule effective date. The guidance addresses a previously ambiguous category of entries where importers paid partial IEEPA duties during the phase-in period.

The supplemental guidance confirms that importers of record who paid any IEEPA-assessed duty on qualifying HTS codes between October 2024 and February 24, 2026 may be eligible for Phase 2 recovery — even if the duty rate was below the full Liberation Day schedule. ACE/ACH enrollment remains required for all claimants regardless of phase.

Impact Expands the eligible importer population. Importers who previously believed they fell outside Phase 1 thresholds should request a new eligibility review.
Apr 29 2026
CBP
CAPE Portal Now Live Inside ACE — Phase 1 Declarations Processing as of April 29

Following the April 20 soft launch, CBP confirmed on April 29 that the CAPE (Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries) portal is fully operational for Phase 1 IEEPA refund declarations. The portal is accessible through the ACE Secure Data Portal and requires active ACE enrollment with a valid ACH bank account on file.

CBP has stated that valid Phase 1 declarations will receive acknowledgment within 5–7 business days and that payment processing is expected within 60–90 days of acceptance. As of the launch date, an estimated 92% of the 330,000 eligible importers have not yet enrolled in ACE/ACH — the threshold requirement for receiving any refund payment.

Impact The refund window is officially open. ACE/ACH enrollment is the critical path blocker for the majority of eligible importers. This must be completed before any CAPE Declaration can be filed or processed.
Apr 15 2026
Court Alert
DOJ Files for Extension; Appeal Deadline Set at June 7, 2026 — Partial Stay Denied

The Department of Justice filed a motion for a 90-day administrative extension of the February 24 court order deadline, citing the complexity of implementing a government-wide refund mechanism of this scale. The court granted a partial extension to June 7, 2026 — roughly half the requested period — while denying the government's request for a stay pending appeal.

The denial of the stay is significant: it means CBP was required to open the CAPE portal and begin processing refunds without waiting for appeal resolution. However, the June 7 date remains the window within which the government may file a further appeal or seek emergency relief from a higher court, which could potentially pause or modify the refund program.

Impact June 7 is the definitive near-term risk horizon. Importers with CAPE Declarations accepted before that date are in a substantially stronger position regardless of subsequent government action.
Feb 24 2026
Court
SCOTUS Rules IEEPA Tariffs Unconstitutional — Learning Resources Inc. v. Trump

The U.S. Supreme Court issued a landmark ruling in Learning Resources Inc. v. Trump, holding that the executive branch's imposition of IEEPA reciprocal tariffs — including the April 2025 "Liberation Day" schedule and all related tariff actions under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act — exceeded statutory authority and was constitutionally invalid.

The Court's majority opinion held that IEEPA does not grant the President open-ended authority to impose tariffs as a tool of economic leverage, and that the tariff actions taken between April 5, 2025 and the ruling date constituted an unlawful delegation of congressional taxing power. The decision ordered CBP to establish a refund mechanism — which became the CAPE portal — for the estimated $100 billion to $175 billion in duties paid during the affected period.

The ruling explicitly excludes Section 232 tariffs (steel, aluminum), Section 301 China tariffs, and the Section 122 tariff signed February 20, 2026 from the refund pool. Only IEEPA reciprocal tariffs imposed between April 5, 2025 and February 24, 2026 are covered.

Foundation This ruling is the legal basis for the entire IEEPA refund program. Every CAPE Declaration, every ACE enrollment, and every advisory engagement The Tariff Bureau provides flows from this decision.
Aug 12 2025
Legislation Alert
DOJ–DHS Trade Fraud Task Force Launches; False Claims Act Enforcement Formally Expanded to Customs Filings

The Department of Justice and Department of Homeland Security jointly announced the formation of a cross-agency Trade Fraud Task Force with an expanded mandate to apply False Claims Act enforcement to customs declarations, including IEEPA tariff filings and, prospectively, any CAPE Declarations submitted under the refund program.

The Task Force operates a machine learning system trained on CBP's database of over 53 million annual shipments to flag classification anomalies, valuation inconsistencies, and country-of-origin mismatches. The announcement confirmed that qui tam relators — whistleblowers including competitors, customs brokers, and former employees — are eligible for up to 30% of government recovery on successful FCA actions. The largest prior customs FCA settlement stands at $54.4 million.

Risk Every CAPE Declaration is a certified legal document. Importers with classification errors, valuation discrepancies, or undocumented transshipment in their 2025–2026 entries face material FCA exposure. Pre-submission audit is strongly recommended for any claim exceeding $250,000.
