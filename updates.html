Urgent May 27, 2026

Nearly 150,000 Importers Facing CAPE Rejection — And Most Don't Know It Yet

Since the CBP CAPE portal opened on April 20, 2026, tens of thousands of IEEPA refund declarations have failed validation — many from importers who believed their filings were complete. CBP data shows that approximately 37% of all CAPE declarations submitted failed file-level validation, meaning the entire submission was rejected before a single entry was reviewed.

330K Eligible importers who paid IEEPA duties
$166B Total IEEPA duties paid — eligible for refund
37% Of CAPE declarations failed validation as of April 26

The most common reasons for rejection include formatting errors in the CSV upload (hidden spaces, improper entry number format), entries outside the 80-day liquidation window, missing IEEPA HTS codes, and mismatches between the filer's ACE account and the Importer of Record on file. When a declaration fails file-level validation, ACE rejects the entire submission — not just individual entries.

⏳
The 80-Day Rolling Window Is Closing Every Day

CAPE Phase 1 covers unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation. Entries that liquidated in February and March 2026 may already be outside this window. Importers with liquidated entries have a 180-day protest window under 19 U.S.C. §1514 — but that clock is running.

For importers who are not U.S. customs brokers, navigating ACE validation rules, entry classification, and liquidation timelines is a technical and procedural challenge. The consequences of a missed or rejected filing are significant: once the protest window closes, CBP has no obligation to issue a refund, regardless of the amount owed.

Prior Updates
CAPE Update May 12, 2026

First CAPE Refund Payments Begin — What Approved Filers Need to Know

The U.S. Department of Treasury began issuing the first ACH refund payments for approved IEEPA CAPE declarations on May 12, 2026. Refunds are consolidated — a single ACH payment covers all approved entries within a declaration — and include applicable statutory interest. Only importers with valid ACH banking information enrolled in ACE are eligible to receive payments.

  • ACH bank enrollment in ACE is required before any refund can be issued
  • The REV-603 Trade Refund report in ACE tracks successful refunds
  • The REV-613 ACH Rejected Refunds report shows any ACH failures due to missing enrollment
  • CBP estimates 60–90 days from declaration acceptance to payment for most approved claims
Portal Alert April 28, 2026

CBP Files CIT Status Update: 75,306 Declarations Submitted in First Week

On April 28, 2026, CBP Executive Director Brandon Lord filed a status declaration with the U.S. Court of International Trade confirming that, as of April 26, over 75,000 CAPE declarations had been submitted covering more than 11 million entries — approximately 21% of total eligible entries. Of those, roughly 63% passed file validation. CBP confirmed that approximately 1.74 million validated entries had already been liquidated and entered the refund processing queue.

  • 75,306 CAPE declarations submitted as of April 26, 2026
  • 47,315 passed file validation (~63%); approximately 28,000 were rejected outright
  • 11.2 million entries covered — roughly 21% of total eligible entries
  • 1.74 million entries already liquidated and in refund processing
CAPE Launch April 20, 2026

CAPE Phase 1 Goes Live — The 80-Day Window Starts Now

U.S. Customs and Border Protection officially launched CAPE Phase 1 on April 20, 2026 at 8:00 a.m. ET, opening the first electronic pathway for IEEPA tariff refund claims. Phase 1 is limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation. Additional phases will expand eligibility to reconciliation entries, drawback claims, and historically liquidated entries later in 2026. Entries subject to antidumping or countervailing duties are excluded from Phase 1.

  • Submissions require a CSV file (CBP template) uploaded via the CAPE tab in ACE
  • Each CAPE Declaration limited to 9,999 entries — multiple submissions allowed
  • No supporting documentation required at time of submission
  • Only the Importer of Record or the authorized customs broker who filed the entries may submit
Background February 2026

Supreme Court Rules IEEPA Tariffs Unlawful — $166 Billion Refund Universe Opens

In February 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court held that tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) were unlawful. The ruling covered tariffs paid beginning February 4, 2025 (fentanyl-related) and April 5, 2025 (reciprocal tariffs), as well as tariffs related to Venezuela, Brazil, and Russia. Subsequent Court of International Trade orders directed CBP to liquidate and reliquidate entries without IEEPA duties and to develop a scalable refund mechanism — which became CAPE. An estimated 330,000 importers paid approximately $166 billion in IEEPA duties across more than 53 million entries.
